What if every shark on Earth vanished tomorrow? Sharks sit at the top of the ocean's food chain, but their real power is not eating. It is fear.

Off the coast of America, scientists watched the chain reaction happen. Big sharks declined, and cownose rays exploded to around forty million. The rays helped wipe out so many bay scallops that a century-old fishery collapsed completely.

In Shark Bay, Australia, tiger sharks do not even need to hunt. Just being nearby scares turtles and dugongs away from the seagrass. That seagrass stores carbon and shelters baby fish.

Oceanic sharks and rays have already fallen seventy-one percent since nineteen seventy. Take the rest away, and the dominoes keep falling. And the fish we eat depend on that balance too.

No sharks, no balance. The ocean's scariest animal turns out to be its guardian.
