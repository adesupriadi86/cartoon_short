You take one big gulp of an ice-cold drink, and suddenly your forehead explodes. That is a brain freeze, and it is your brain trying to save itself.

The cold hits the roof of your mouth, where blood vessels sit just under the skin. They slam shut to keep the cold out.

But your brain is picky about temperature. It panics and orders warm blood to rush up. The vessels fly open again, and the pressure spike trips the pain nerves around them.

Here is the weird part. Those nerves report through the trigeminal nerve, the same cable that serves your forehead. So your brain gets the wires crossed, and feels the pain in your forehead instead of your mouth. Doctors call that referred pain.

The fix is simple. Press your warm tongue to the roof of your mouth. The vessels calm down, the signal stops, and your brain goes back to guard duty.
