Look at the window when you fly. It's round. That little curve is why the plane stays in one piece.
Every jet is a metal balloon. Up high, the air is thin, so the cabin is pumped full of air to keep you breathing.
That pressure pushes on the skin, over and over, every flight.
Cut a square hole in that skin, and all the force piles up in the four sharp corners.
Tiny cracks start there. And with every takeoff and landing, they grow.
In 1954, two Comet jets broke apart in mid-air. The cracks had started at the corners of the square windows.
So engineers changed the shape. No corners, no pile-up. The stress spreads smoothly around the curve.
That's why every window today is round. Not a style choice. A lesson written in metal.
